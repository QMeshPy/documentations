QMesh is an open-source research platform that treats quantum operations as **network services**. A coordinator discovers worker peers over libp2p, plans a circuit into fragments, reserves capacity on the peers, runs the fragments, and returns the result with a **receipt** you can verify.

<Callout type="warn" title="What is real, and what is simulated">
  Circuits run on Qiskit statevector **simulation**, not quantum hardware. The network, payments and
  provenance are real, and every chain QMesh writes to is a **testnet**.
</Callout>

## How a request flows

Every run passes through the same five layers.

<Steps>
  <Step>
    ### Your app

    Submit an OpenQASM circuit to the REST API, or paste it in the operator console.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Coordinator

    Normalize the circuit, build the execution plan, and split it into fragments.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Payments and registry

    Answer `402 Payment Required`, settle in USDC, and look up the chains and services involved.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Peer mesh

    Reserve peer capacity and stream fragments over libp2p for Qiskit statevector simulation.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Provenance

    Hash the inputs, plan and results into a bundle, pin it to IPFS, and anchor the Merkle root.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The full walk-through, with sequence diagrams and state machines, is in [Architecture](/docs/architecture).

## The headline result

97% of QAOA runtime is the classical COBYLA parameter search, not the quantum circuit, so adding quantum peers to one run yields at most a **1.03×** speedup (Amdahl's law). The advantage shows up in scaling: **3.2×** faster at 40 assets and **9.5×** at 60, in simulator benchmarks from the [research paper](/docs/research/paper).
