QMesh is an open-source research platform that treats quantum operations as **network services**. You submit an
[OpenQASM](https://openqasm.com) circuit to a coordinator. The coordinator discovers worker peers over libp2p,
splits the circuit into fragments, reserves capacity on the peers, runs the fragments, and returns the result
together with a provenance record you can verify.

<Callout type="warn" title="Simulated qubits">
  QMesh runs circuits on Qiskit statevector **simulation**. It does not control quantum hardware. The network,
  the job lifecycle, the payment flow and the provenance records are real software, and every blockchain it
  uses is a **testnet**. See [Scope](/docs/scope) for the full picture.
</Callout>

## What you can do with it

- **Run a circuit across peers.** Submit OpenQASM over HTTP and watch the job move from `QUEUED` to `COMPLETED`.
  See [Basic usage](/docs/basic-usage).
- **Optimize a portfolio.** Upload asset data and compare a QAOA run with classical baselines.
- **Price options and measure risk.** Run option-pricing and risk jobs (VaR, CVaR, credit risk) through the same
  job model.
- **Pay per request.** Expose a paid endpoint with the [x402](/docs/guides/x402-payments) `402 Payment Required`
  flow, settled in USDC on Algorand TestNet.
- **Verify a result.** Every completed circuit job gets a provenance bundle with a Merkle root. See
  [Provenance](/docs/concepts/provenance).

## How a request flows

A request passes through five layers. Each one has its own page under Concepts.

<Iso scene="stack" />

<Steps>
  <Step>
    ### Your app

    Submit an OpenQASM circuit to the REST API, or paste it into the operator console.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Coordinator

    Normalize the circuit, build an execution plan and split it into fragments.
    See [Circuit planning](/docs/concepts/circuit-planning).
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Payments and registry

    Charge for the request, either from prepaid credits or through an x402 payment, and look up the services
    that are available.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Peer mesh

    Reserve capacity on each peer, then stream the fragments to them over libp2p.
    See [Peers and discovery](/docs/concepts/peers) and [Reservations](/docs/concepts/reservations).
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Provenance

    Hash the inputs, the plan and the fragment results into a bundle with a Merkle root.
  </Step>
</Steps>
